# Plan the requested motion

Choose cycle, oneshot-return or oneshot-hold from intended gameplay behavior. Describe actual facing, pose, expression, local movement, support constraints, attachments and motion envelope without selecting a named template. Confirm only consequential ambiguity. A first frame does not guarantee the generated movement.

## Frame the complete motion envelope

Use centred framing as a starting default, not a constraint. Estimate the union of occupied space over the whole animation, including the body, held objects, clothing and every attached or trailing part at their maximum extension in every direction. Include intended local translation and safety margins on all sides; neither the initial silhouette nor the main movement direction alone defines the required space.

Choose the starting position and directional free space to contain that complete envelope at a consistent character scale. Do not prescribe a fixed fraction of the canvas. If clearance is insufficient, first shift the starting position and redistribute free space, checking that the opposite side also remains clear. If the whole envelope still cannot fit, reconcile the canvas or shared scale with the confirmed specification before generation; never silently crop, squash or shrink one motion to conceal insufficient space.

Inspect the prepared input before submission, then verify the complete generated candidate across all frames. An estimate is not proof of clearance. Reject source clipping even when the starting pose fits. First distinguish missing source pixels from clipping introduced by registration or export: repair the latter locally using the intact source rather than submitting another generation. Before a source-clipping retake, tell the user which part and direction were cut, how position and margins will change, then submit without waiting for approval, subject to the repeated-deviation checkpoint below. Recheck all directions after shifting; ordinary position/margin adjustment does not authorize changing accepted resolution, playback duration or shared character scale. Automatic registration padding can extend a logical canvas once for intact source pixels; it cannot restore pixels cut off by the generator.

Carry the planned placement, margins and consistent scale through registration, finishing and delivery. Final cells need not centre the character or the occupied bounds. Do not independently recentre, trim or rescale frames; inspect the final-size animation to ensure the full envelope remains intact. Treat canvas-fit warnings as a decision to review and resolve, not permission to silently change the planned scale.

Decide whether the engine or animation owns world movement. Distinguish intended local motion from unwanted drift before applying positional locks or stabilisation; preserve the movement needed to perform and recover from the action. Prepare separate phases/references only when the request needs them.

Source2-3 repetitions provide selection opportunities for all three types. They are not a longer single action or a combination of unrelated movements. Compare full independent candidates; retain one complete instance including its correct ending. A continuous period matches moving phase, a returning action completes recovery, and a held-ending action retains its terminal state. If repeat count compromises completeness, propose more source time.

Try flat-background extraction independently of motion type. Preserve true alpha. Estimate arbitrary solid input colour from four edges, check consistency and the resulting matte, then compare generation keys against subject colours. Continue if successful; stop for a user-corrected image only if extraction/quality fails. Never use colour locking to disguise alpha loss. Review output transparency on light/dark backgrounds.

Choose timing after candidate review. Cycles usually need phase selection; one-shots normally need retime. Explicit key poses, holds and events follow the request and observed source, not an action label. Keep original sequence order and preserve intended initial/terminal state. Review the final-size materialized result before acceptance.

## Repeated-deviation checkpoint

When TWO consecutive completed, reviewed generation attempts show the SAME deviation from an active requirement, pause BEFORE the third submission. This applies to any motion or unwanted phenomenon, not an action-specific list. Count separate generated takes, not repeated demonstrations within one take, frames, polling or resuming an existing job. A changed prompt or seed does not erase the observed repeat. This checkpoint also applies to repeated source clipping and takes precedence over routine framing-retake autonomy.

Tell the user what was observed, which requirement it violates, and what was changed between the attempts (including if only the seed changed). Clearly separate evidence from prediction: repeated failure does not prove that the requested result is impossible. State that, based on the current results, another submission would likely reproduce the same issue. In Traditional Chinese this may be phrased:「依目前結果推測，再次重送大概率仍可能發生。」Localize to the user's language; substitute the actual phenomenon and requirement, never a canned action example.

Offer numbered choices:1 relax that specific condition;2 keep the original requirement and continue trying. Wait for the user's decision before submitting again. If they relax it, record the exact accepted change and update the acceptance criteria; do not infer permission to relax other requirements or retroactively claim an unapproved take was compliant. If they insist, retain the original criteria and use a different attempt method. Modifying the prompt and changing the random seed EACH count as different methods; they may be combined, and changing pose/framing/reference is not mandatory for every retry. Tell the user what will change before the next submission. Do not declare the task impossible solely because of two matching failures, silently lower the standard or continue endless submissions without this decision.

Use the workflow's supported controls. New jobs already receive a new recorded random seed when a seed socket exists; a seed-only retake is valid and should be described as such. Preserve the frozen seed and hashes when resuming a pending/unknown job or reusing a completed take; never edit an existing job or resubmit it to change its seed. Workflows without seed control require another available method, not an invented flag.
